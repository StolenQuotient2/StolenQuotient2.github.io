While the other five continents in Panops have relatively calm, flat topography, Sheol is a staggering exception to this pattern. The largest of the six islands, Sheol is home to an incredibly diverse set of biomes and landforms, the exact details of which are described in \[Geography and Topography\]. Because of this, Sheol is by far the most dangerous island to traverse alone, which does not even count the severe and quickly changing weather patterns on the island, which are maintained by \[The Veil\]. Thus, Sheol is not a suitable place for vacation, but is an enticing place for young adventurers who wish to test their mettle. 

The [Kaelo archipelago]({% link obsidian/Kaelo.md %}) resides off its coast.

#### **Competitive Adventuring**

Because of the danger of Sheol, the Church has turned the island into a host for Panops’ largest public sport. Referred to as “The Games” by most, the sport involves questing through Sheol, and completing Church-built dungeons for points, as well as monetary and magical rewards.   
	Each dungeon has a core goal–Slay a boss, solve a puzzle, find a secret–and once that goal is completed, the dungeon will present rewards to the participants, and then reset fully when they exit. Rewards come in the form of Coin, magical items or components, and Tokens, which are the scoring system for the Games. Tokens can be freely traded and used as adventurer-specific currency, or they can be submitted to the Church and added to a team’s point total.   
While a party can complete the same dungeon any number of times, they can only do the same dungeon once per day, and more difficult dungeons present higher levels of rewards.   
At the end of each calendar year, the 10 teams with the highest number of submitted tokens are presented with specialized rewards given by the God-King himself. Historically these are custom-built magic items tailored to the specific teams. The top 3 teams are given trophies as well, and are given the option to retire from the games. Teams who do are provided with a variety of luxuries in perpetuity by the Church, but are forbidden from reentering the Games.    

#### **Geography and Topography**  {#geography-and-topography}

- The Northeast

The northeasternmost parts of the island are populated by some of the calmer landscapes of Sheol; primarily forests and plains, and relatively mild weather. You should still expect heavy rainfall, and be wary of lightning in the open clearings. The Northeast contains a good assortment of dungeon difficulties. There are both introductory and advanced dungeons in this area, depending how deep into the forests you go, but the area consists primarily of introductory dungeons. 

- The North

The central-north area of the island, while on land has many of the same characteristics as the Northeast, has primarily coastal and oceanic dungeons of medium difficulty. While the oceans of Panops are generally calm, be wary of artificial weather off the coast that represents the area where a dungeon lies. Some dungeons in this area are only revealed during low/high tide. 

- The West

In stark contrast to the grassy regions of the North/Northeast, the West is one large desert. Dungeons of various difficulties reside here, though many are on the upper end of difficulty. Still, it's far easier to find an introductory dungeon in this area than a raincloud. 

- The South

The South is the most dangerous part of the island. Active volcanoes and lava flows permeate this mountainous region, where steep climbs and lethal terrain add to the already extreme difficulty of the dungeons in this area. The South area of Sheol is not for the faint of heart–there are no introductory dungeons in this area whatsoever. 

- The East

The East is a beautiful cascade of various magically-enhanced terrains. Floating spires of rock, vibrant flower fields, skyscraper-tall trees. Dungeons in this area settle very nicely in medium-difficulty territory.
