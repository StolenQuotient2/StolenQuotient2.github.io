Counterspells are a large class of spells that are designed to interfere with the operations of other spells. In *Misfits* there is no “spell that counters”, but rather several ways of countering spells using a wide range of different spells. This section goes into the five ways, and their differences. 

- Obliteration of a spell is when you destroy the spell before it can take full effect. Best examples of these are destroying projectile-based spells with Wreck spells while they are still in flight.   
	- Obliteration is a generally very selective method of countering a spell. It works on only very specific conditions and on specific spells. Obliteration specifically only works on spells that have not taken full effect, and can thus be very restrictive.  
- Nullification of a spell is when you cast a spell with the inverse effect. The best examples are reversing Aid spells with Hinder spells, and Hinder spells with Aid spells.   
	- Nullification can still be somewhat restrictive, since you need to find a spell that operates as a functional inverse. Some effects don’t have obvious reverse effects.   
	- Nullification technically refers to *non-trivial inversion*, where the second spell is not declared to simply invert the first. For example, a fire spell and a water spell are non-trivial inversions, but a fire spell and a “not-fire” spell are trivial inversions.   
- Deconstruction of a spell involves destroying some of the internal processes of the spell itself, resulting in the spell failing to function. To clarify the distinction with Obliteration, Deconstruction typically requires some underlying knowledge of the operations of the spell, and generally only applies to spells with continuous effects. 
- Subsumption of a spell is when you cast a spell that completely engulfs the first spell. While mages typically envision this as "snuffing" a spell, like you would a candle, the metaphysical mechanics behind this effect actually involve simply casting a larger spell in the same place as the original spell. 
	- Subsuming a spell requires being able to cover the entire area of effect of a spell, and is much better suited to small spells. If a small part of the area of the spell is not covered, the spell will not be subsumed.
