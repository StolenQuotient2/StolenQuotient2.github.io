Recess is the less threatening phase of play, where the party can relax and complete various tasks outside the realm of imminent threats and action rolls. 

After an encounter ends, the party can choose to take Recess, and if so, for how long. The length of Recess determines the number of *Beats* the party receives, which can be spent on *Measures* to do tasks during Recess. Below is a table of various Recess periods and the associated number of Beats. 

| Duration            | Beats                   |
| :------------------ | :---------------------- |
| A few hours or less | 0                       |
| Up to a day         | 6                       |
| Up to a week        | 12                      |
| Up to a month       | 24                      |
| Longer than a month | See \[Extended Recess\] |

Regardless of the length of Recess, the first thing every character does each Recess is lower their injury level by 1, remove the Severely Injured status, restore all of their mana, and restore 1 Focus. Additional restoration requires the use of Measures. 

## **Measures** 

**Rest**  
Costs 2 Beats. The player taking Rest reduces their injury level by 1\.  
**Recover**  
Costs 4 Beats. The player taking Recover resets their injury level to None.    
**Breathe**  
Costs 1 Beat. The player taking Breathe restores all mana.   
**Center**  
Costs 2 Beats. The player taking Center restores 1 Focus.   
**Meditate**  
Costs 4 Beats. The player taking Meditate restores all Focus. 

**Advance**
Costs 1 Beat. Spend EXP to gain [Advancements]({% link obsidian/Player_Advancement.md %}). The player taking Advance may gain any number of advancements when taking this measure, provided they have sufficient EXP. 

**Acquire**  
Costs X Beats. Describe an item, and then spend a number of beats equal to the rarity value (1 for common, 2 for uncommon, etc.) of the item if it is reasonable to acquire (GM discretion). Then describe how you are getting access to the item. If you are buying the item, spend Coin equal to the cost of the item. If you are acquiring the item in some other way, spend beats equal to the rarity value of the item again. Cast any spells necessary to do so.   
**Sell**  
Costs X Beats. Choose what items you are selling, and spend a number of beats equal to the rarity value of the item. Gain Coin equal to the sell price of the item. The sell price of the item may be increased at GM discretion if the item is highly enchanted, especially unique, or otherwise special. The sell price is generally capped at the buy price of the item, which is 2 to the power of the rarity (ie. Common 2, Uncommon 4, Rare 8…)  
**Search**  
Costs X Beats. Describe a person or place, then spend a number of beats between 1-5 to GM’s discretion, based on how well-known the target is and any specific connections the party has to the target (a well-known target should only cost 1 Beat). You gain true and actionable information about the whereabouts of the target, as well as any reasonable paths to get there.   
**Identify**  
Costs X Beats, where X is the rarity of the item, or potency of the spell. (Minor or Common is 1, Major or Uncommon is 2, etc,). You learn the intricate details of a spell or magic item you are near or in possession of.   
You may also use this Measure for 1 Beat to gain intricate details about the origin or material of a nonmagical item you are in possession of.   
**Connect**  
Costs X Beats. Describe a person or organization, then spend a number of beats between 1-5 to the GM’s discretion, based on how easy the target is to contact, and how willing they are to talk to you. You can send a message to this target or organize a short meeting, and they will respond or attend accordingly.   
**Work**  
Costs X Beats. Gain 1 Coin for every Beat spent.   
**Hide**  
Costs 4 Beats. Protect yourself and your team from being found in this Recess. Describe how you’re doing so, and cast any spells necessary to do so. 

### ***Extended Recess***


The Reapers typically ask their members to contribute to a beneficial task to the Reapers approximately once per month. Normally, this is considered to be done by the players at an arbitrary convenient time and does not appear in play. However, if the players are going to take Recess for a long period of time, they must make concerted efforts to contribute to the Reapers’ infrastructure and goals.   
Start with a standard 1-month recess, gaining 24 Beats and executing Measures. Then, you may decide to Recess for another month. If you do, each player is assigned a task by the Reapers which takes a \[Skill\] to complete. The associated level of the task starts at the party level, and then increases by 1 for each time this cycle repeats. Every player who succeeds on their roll contributes 6 Beats, and can participate in Measures. Every player who fails their roll contributes 0 Beats, and cannot participate in Measures for the entire month. Then, if at least one player succeeded on their task, you may choose to Recess for another month and repeat the process. Excess Beats carry over between months in an Extended Recess.   
If all players fail to complete their task, this process ends, and no more Beats can be earned or Measures can be taken. Furthermore, each player must choose one of the following: take a Major Injury, set your mana to 0, or lose half your Focus. 

## **The World Keeps Turning**

While you take Recess, *the World Keeps Turning*. The NPCs and factions outside of the party’s immediate view continue to act on whatever plans and goals they had previously. At the end of Recess, the party takes stock of what has transpired during the time away.  
Depending on the amount of time spent in Recess, Factions or NPCs can complete Measures of their own.   
If you took up to a day of Recess, the Measures should be None-Minor. The same goes for a week and Minor-Major, and a month and Major-Extreme. Any longer constitutes an Extreme/Lethal Measure being executed.   
Your GM will decide what the Measures look like. These Measures can either be directly interacting with the party and the party’s plans, or simple happenings in the world that have come to the party’s attention for one reason or another. These are NOT Threats; they *Just Happen*. The party can choose to address them after the fact, or not once Free Play begins again.
