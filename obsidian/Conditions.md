## Conditions

Rather than a health point total, each character can only suffer a certain number of *Conditions* before they are taken out of play. Conditions represent both injuries as well as other hinderances on the player characters. They range from minor problems, such as being distracted, to severe and life-threatening problems, like stab wounds. 

Each condition comes with a *Level* between 1 and 3. When a player takes 6 (or more) total levels of conditions, they are removed from play, and suffer [Shock, Trauma, or Death]. Conditions can have two additional modifiers: they can be *Injuries*, and they can be *Severe*. Both modifiers we will discuss later. 

### Applying Conditions

Conditions arise in play as a natural consequence of the narrative--when you fail actions, trigger traps, or otherwise get into bad situations, your GM may apply a condition to you. If you're tangled in vines, maybe its a Restrained (Level 2). If a firework went off in your face, maybe its Singed (Level 1, Injury) and Blinded (Level 2). If you're still trying to flirt with that cute girl from the [Talents]({% link obsidian/Talents.md %}) section, then maybe its Flustered (Level 1000). 

When you are given a condition, write down its description and level on your character sheet. When you attempt to take actions, any relevant conditions you have apply a -1 Fate penalty to your action. 
Note: not all conditions will be relevant to all actions. If you're restrained, you are going to have a hard time casting spells, but you may still be able to talk your way out of a situation without being impeded. Only apply the fate penalty for relevant conditions. 


## Removing Conditions

Most Conditions can be removed as part of the narrative of the story. If you're restrained, and a player cuts you lose, lose the relevant condition and any levels it was contributing. If you were blinded by the sun, and you look away and take the time to adjust, lose the relevant condition. et cetera. Injuries and Severe conditions have special rules for removal, as outlined below. 

Conditions, in general, often require an Action to remove. Some conditions may resolve as part of other actions, some conditions may require actions of their own, but very few conditions resolve themselves. 


### *Severe Conditions*

Your GM may designate a condition as *Severe*. A Severe Condition requires multiple steps to remove--it cannot be completely mitigated in a single action. When you take a Severe Condition, mark its Level and Description, and also mark that it is Severe. When a first action is taken to mitigate the condition, remove the Severe label first. Only once the severe label has been removed can the rest of the condition be removed. 

E.g. You may be tied with multiple ropes. This condition may be Restrained (Level 2, Severe). If a player takes an action to remove the ropes, they can cut many of the ropes, but not make it all the way through, reducing the condition to Restrained (Level 2). A second action will then be needed to remove the condition fully. 

### *Injuries*

Injuries are a special kind of condition that are, well, injuries. Injuries cannot typically be removed during the course of a normal situation. They require time and precise care to be removed--removing an Injury during an encounter requires the Medical/Medicinal/{Whatever its called} Talent. Most injuries are cleared during Recess, where the level of an injury you have is reduced by 1 passively, but can be reduced further using Measures. 

Injuries can also be Severe. Severe injuries, like any severe condition, take an additional instance of reduction to fully heal. Remove the "Severe" status before any level reductions. In addition,  Severe injuries cannot be healed during a Situation, even by someone with Medical talent. They must be addressed during Free play/Recess. 

### Shock, Trauma, and Death. 

When a player receives 6 or more total levels of Conditions, they are removed from play. They are still narratively in the current Situation, but cannot take actions. In addition, depending on the number of Injuries that player had taken, the character suffers one of the three: Shock, Trauma, or Death. 

Note: if you have Shock, you can still experience Trauma. If you experience Trauma, you can still experience Death. These are ascending levels of severity. 

### *Shock*

If you take 6 or more levels of Conditions, and your most recent condition was not an Injury, you experience *Shock*. Your character cannot act until they have been completely cleared of conditions. Often this will involve getting to Recess, and handling those conditions then. While experiencing Shock, you can continue to accrue conditions. You cannot act until those conditions have been removed as well. 

{Remark: I (Sam) may apply the condition Shock (Level 6) as a way to force someone out of play in particularly emotionally traumatic situations. This is a testing condition, but you've now been warned.}

### *Trauma*

If you take 6 or more levels of Conditions, and your most recent condition was an Injury, you experience *Trauma*. Trauma inflicts all the conditions of Shock--you cannot act until you clear all of your conditions. Moreover, the most recent injury you've taken compounds with your current injuries to result in a *permanent* disability of your character. Work with your GM to describe the nature of the Trauma you experience; it can be either physical or mental. Similar to a Condition, anytime your Trauma might cause you to struggle in an action you are taking, take a -1 to your roll. Your Trauma stays with you for the rest of your campaign. 

Each time you take an injury while experiencing Shock or Trauma, you may experience Trauma again, up until you experience Death. (So, up to a total of 4 Traumas per trauma.)

### *Death* 

If you have 6 or more levels of Conditions, and 4 or more levels of them are Injuries, you experience *Death*. Your character dies. Their body remains in place, and their soul passes to the World Beyond Death. You cannot act while dead. You remain dead until a [Resurrection]({% link obsidian/Death_and_Resurrection.md %}) is performed, or forever. 

When a player dies, each other member of the party that witnessed the person die takes a Level 3 Severe condition. A member of the party who learns about the death in any other way takes a Level 2 Severe condition. This condition represents the cognitive impairment from losing a friend.
