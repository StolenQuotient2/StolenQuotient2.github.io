Around 15 years ago, an epidemic began. It’s not well-known where this disease came from, but very quickly, it was everywhere. The illness became known as the Drain for two reasons. One, for the intense lethargic, energyless feeling it gives as its primary symptom, and Two, because unlike a typical virus, which is parasitic towards its host, the Drain draws mana from nearby sources in order to replicate. This makes it an incredibly strange pathogen, as it's transmitted by magic.   

The amount of mana the Drain uses is small. This is a negligible quantity for anyone who is magically inclined; it's trivial to draw that much mana and keep the Drain fed, so trivial that those who can do so involuntarily. However, for those who are not magically inclined, the only source of magic in their body is their soul, which the Drain eats away at, causing overwhelming lethargy. This makes the Drain a very slow killer, and one that disproportionately affects people without magical talent.   

Many, many people died. The nature of the drain made it very hard to combat, because mundane medicine couldn’t affect it, and arcane methods tended to be ineffective or made the illness worse. A variety of alternative methods to remove the illness or to abate its symptoms did not work either, often resulting in their own disastrous consequences.  So, with nothing else to turn to, the people of Panops prayed to Aries, begging him to rid the world of this illness.   

The Church provided lots of support during the crisis, mobilizing its personal resources in order to build new hospitals, providing food to families, warning people of the crisis, etc. However, Aries himself did not help, in spite of the overwhelming amount of prayer coming from the population of Panops. In an announcement He broadcast as far as He could, Aries explained that were He to use magic to try to abolish the Drain, the Drain would simply feed off the mana and not be destroyed. Therefore, he must stay his hand.   

The world was split on this explanation. Many panopsians took this explanation in stride, and appreciated the Church’s efforts to provide whatever nonmagical resources it could. Many more took starker opinions on the announcement.   

In the following months, resentment towards the God-King skyrocketed. His inability to act in the largest crisis since the start of the Golden Age caused a massive loss in faith, so strong that large-scale protests against the God-King broke out. Many of the protests were so extreme that Enforcement was dispatched against the population and protesters were charged with crimes as serious as heresy.   

The Revolts eventually fizzled out due to a combination of factors, but primarily from the arrest or death of the majority of the strongest protesters. While the Revolts ended, the resentment toward the God-King was still alive, primarily due to the children of Panops. For an unknown reason, young children (\~15 years old or younger) have a much higher probability of being magically inclined, and thus were more likely to survive the Drain…while their parents didn't. 

The large scale of displaced children was a problem everywhere, and the typical strategy for smaller cities was to bring displaced children to the capital city of Wei-Jin, which had the largest and most successful foster care centers anywhere on Panops. Unfortunately, Wei-Jin’s foster care system was overwhelmed, and this left a surplus of orphaned children in the capital city specifically.   

In an attempt to resolve this problem, a man and his son registered a charity organization with the intent of housing and teaching orphaned children. The two of them bought an abandoned Dreamer school and used it as their first location near the center of Wei-Jin, which was soon full of kids of all ages. They also named the organization “The Reapers”, in an attempt to give it a cool name to attract young kids. The man who started the orphanage died soon after from the Drain, but his son, Vanta, took over operation of the Reapers, and dubbed the first location Alpha.
