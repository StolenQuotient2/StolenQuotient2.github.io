*"...and on this day I give to you my word of law; the beliefs I urge you to hold dearest to your heart."* --The God-King, announcing the Lex Dei at the beginning of Humanity's First Golden Age. 

---

The *Lex Dei* is the central document which enumerates the laws of Panops, as issued by the Church and Aries Himself. {Lex Dei translates roughly to "law of god"}. The Lex Dei functions both as a concrete legal document, and as a set of divine commandments. Because of this, the Lex Dei is not a very long document, and legal arguments in Panops will occasionally decend into an argument of interpretation of the Lex Dei.

The Lex Dei below gives the commandment itself as a bullet point, and the official (as in, they have been decreed by Aries as part of the document) interpretations of the commandment. The terms in parentheticals are further clarifications about the laws and their interpretation, especially common ones, as well as any notable details about the law, its creation, or stories regarding it. 

The order below is based on the decreeing of these descriptions, and laws of the Lex Dei are cited by number in this order (e.g. "Lex Dei 1.2" refers to the right to bodily autonomy). 

1. **The Self is Inviolable.** This provides:
	1. *A right to self-defense*. The Church does not convict reasonable acts of violence committed in response to being attacked. 
		1. ("Reasonable" force is qualified typically in the sense of 'equal measure'. A fist can be answered by a fist, but not by an explosion. Juries have consistently given a bit of leeway to the defender in terms of what "Reasonable" counts as, if the amount of force is typically 1. nonlethal and 2. completely stops the attack, or further attacks.)
	2. *A right to bodily autonomy.* A person cannot be forced into any medical procedure, and they have a right to alter their body in whatever ways they see fit; tattoos, piercings, etc. Furthermore, sexual assault and rape are crimes as recognized by the Church. 
		1. (Lex Dei 1.2 has a famous case associated with it, specifically in a Church case against the [Animi], which was ruled that even though the Animi's procedures are extraordinarily violent, they do not violate the bodily autonomy of the subject, as the subject goes into the procedure with full awareness and consent.) <!-- OBSIDIAN COMMENT THAT HAS BEEN REMOVED -->
		2. (Interestingly, in theory you could cite Lex Dei 1.2 in order to object to being  resurrected by someone; however, noone has ever tried this, and it would be strange to be happy about being alive.)
	3. *A right to identity*, in all its forms; gender, sexuality, race, etc. {Putting the DEI in Lex Dei.}
		1. (Lex Dei 1.3 makes major examples of discrimination a Church-recognized crime, and has been utilized as such to bring cases against people and companies for extensive hatred or discrimination. Since the Church takes the stance in general that 'intrapersonal disputes' are not the subject of the Church's judicial system, most small instances of discrimination are not tried.)
	4. *A right to free expression.* 
		1. (The term "free expression" is of hot debate to its meaning, in particular because this condition seems to overlap heavily with other conditions in the Lex Dei, such as 1.2, 1.3, and 3.3. It is sometimes cited in cases involving artistic works, but those often fall better under Lex Dei 3.3 or X.Y)
	5. *A right to life.* The Church does not kill as a punishment for crimes. Killing others in conditions other than self-defense is a crime as recognized by the church.
2. **The God-King is Ineffable.** This condemns:
	1. Heresy
		1. (Heresy concerns specifically religious teachings in contradiction to those of the Church. This is not written in the Lex Dei, but is near-irrefutable as this is the common definition of the word in Panops)
		2. (Possession of "heretical objects" has also been considered violation of Lex Dei 2.2.)
	2. Blasphemy
		1. (Blasphemy concerns specifically defying Aries. In particular, worship of other gods is considered blasphemy, especially those without physical existence. Like Heresy, this description is not written because it is considered common definition.)
		2. (Possession of "blasphemic objects" has also been considered violation of Lex Dei 2.3. However, it is very hard for the Church to utilize this law, since the existence of a blasphemic object is dangerously close to a genuine refutation of Aries' right to rule.)
		3. (This law has only typically been used in cases of verbal blasphemy, written blasphemy, or to protect the Verses of Creation. Anything else is hard to convict for the reason above. Even the Verses of Creation are likely better tried under Lex Dei 2.1)
3. **Truth is a Commitment to Uphold.** This concerns: <!-- OBSIDIAN COMMENT THAT HAS BEEN REMOVED -->
	1. *The right to a fair and fast trial.* 
		1. (The details of how trials are held in Panops are the subject of [The Trial System]) <!-- OBSIDIAN COMMENT THAT HAS BEEN REMOVED -->
	2. *The right to free and honest press.* 
		1. ("honest" is a critical word here. The Church can theoretically convict on cases of libel, but very rarely does.)
	3. *A right to open critique.* The Church does not convict protest or criticism of its processes as a government. 
		1. (Lex Dei 3.3 is a bit controversial due to the Revolts. The convictions related to the Riots relied on establishing that the criticism in the Revolts behaved in the nature of denouncing Aries as a god, or telling that he did not have the people's best interests at heart, violating Lex Dei 2.2 and 2.1 respectively. In the same vein, even very prolific protestors were not able to be convicted because their critiques were ruled to only criticize the governmental responsibilities of the Church, not Aries as a god, thus being permitted under Lex Dei 3.2 and 3.3.)
	4. *An obligation to education.* Persons unable or unwilling to work are expected to be students or teachers in a formal educational setting. 
		1. (This statement is broad. The first clause pertaining "persons unable or unwilling to work" applies mostly to students and the elderly, but also the physically disabled. This is one of the clearer examples of Aries beliefs on personhood and contribution to society in the Lex Dei; a central way to contribute to society is through expanding your own knowledge, and the knowledge of others)
		2. (The term "formal educational setting" has been ruled to be a wide range of things. Schools, master/apprentice relationships, and even dedicated self-study have all been ruled to be formal educational settings. )
		3. (This law is almost exclusively cited in truancy cases.)
		4. (The Lex Dei typically deals in absolute statements; the term "obligation" in Lex Dei 3.4 is strange, but is a direct quotation from Aries and has been reinforced by him as his intended term. Some legal scholars argue that this law is meant to be less severely enforced because of this.)
4. **Noble Actions are to be Protected**. This provides:
	1. *Protection for the Good Samaritan.* The Church will not convict any person who made a best-effort to protect others or prevent them from harm, regardless of the nature of any crimes they may have committed inadvertently in the process.
		1. (According to one popular story, a lawyer once tried to quote Lex Dei X.1 in a blasphemy case; It worked. Whether or not this story is true is unconfirmed)
	2. *Protection for Defending Council*. The Church will not convict any person who provides legal services to a person accused of a crime under the Lex Dei. Furthermore, the Church cannot compel the testimony of a person who has provided legal services to a person accused of a crime under the Lex Dei.
		1. (Lex Dei 4.2 is particularly important for defenses in cases of heresy or blasphemy, where the nature of the defense may imply a heresy or blasphemy charge against the lawyer.)
		2. (Aries decreed the statement of Lex Dei 4.2 without context of which commandment it should be placed under. Church lawyers cite the law as Lex Dei 4.2, creating the convention. Otherwise, it would be under Lex Dei 3.5.)
	3. *Protection for Sanctuaries.* (Lex Dei X.Y is the longest law in the whole Lex Dei, and also the newest. Because of its extensive nature, the laws on Sanctuaries have been denoted below in their own section.)
