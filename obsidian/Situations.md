## **Entering a Situation**


The party exits Free Play and enters a *Situation* when there is some quantifiable threat or chance of failure in the current scene. When a Situation starts, each player gets 2 [Action Points], and may take actions using those resources. As in free play, at the start of the Situation and after each action, the GM will narrate the current environment. In a situation, however, the players will have an explicit beat to identify [Threats]. 

The party exits the current Situation and returns to Free Play as soon as there are no threats left to the players. 

## Identifying and Specifying Threats

During a Situation, any time the GM narrates the events of the current scene, the party will have a chance to *Identify Threats*. The party describes and makes a short list of the most immediate problems they perceive in the current scene. Once the party is satisfied with their list, the GM specifies each Threat with an [Immediacy] value, and then the players may take actions. 

Importantly: the party can fail to identify every Threat in a current situation. The GM should make every effort to narrate the scene in such a way that every threat in the scene is identifiable, but need not make any indication to the players that their list of Threats is complete (e.g. there may be a hidden trap that is only subtly indicated). Similarly, the party may accidentally over-identify threats--"identifying" Threats which do not actually exist in the current scene (e.g. a party member might suspect a trap or ambush when there are none). A Threat which doesn't "actually" exist, should still be treated as a Threat for all mechanical purposes.

Some special abilities and Talents allow the players to automatically identify Threats. If this is the case, the GM should enumerate any Threats that fit the condition of the relevant ability(s). However, these listed Threats need not have specific details (e.g. the Alert Talent automatically identifies any Threat which is an attack on one or more party members--but it does not require that the details of the attack be specified). When an ability says that it *Specifies* a Threat, it both identifies the Threat AND gives specific information about it. 

Identifying and Specifying threats also should remove any over-specified Threats that fall under the conditions of the relevant ability(s). 

## Threat Immediacy

After the players create their list of Threats, the GM will assign a numerical value to each Threat. This number is called the *Immediacy* of the threat. After a player takes a [Standard Action], tick down the Immediacy of each Threat that still applies to the current scene. Any Threats with an Immediacy of 0 then happen, inflicting any relevant consequences on the party. 

As a Situation develops over many actions, the Immediacy of a Threat may change. If it does, change the Immediacy of older Threats before newer ones.


## Action Points

Once Threats have been established, actions during a Situation take the same form as any other kind of action, except with an additional cost of *Action Points* (AP). Each player starts the Situation with 2, and can have a maximum of 4 at any given time. AP costs are listed below. 

Most actions you will take are *Standard Actions*. Whenever a player takes a standard action, each player who did *not* participate in that action gains 1 AP. Players may also take *quick actions*, which do not grant AP, and do not progress Threats' [Immediacy]. 

Below are a list of AP costs associated with various actions. If an action is marked with an asterisk (\*), then it is a Quick Action. Quick actions typically do not require rolls. 

Free Actions (0AP):  
  - \*Communicating and coordinating with party members  
  - \*Short commands or messages to non-party members  
  - \*Walking or running short distances.   
  - Using a \[Prepared\] action that had its condition met this round.  
  - *Waiting* – Do nothing (but trigger the AP bonus for taking a Standard Action and progress the Immediacy of threats). 
  - \*[Quick-Attuning]({% link obsidian/Attuning.md %}). 

Minor Actions (1AP):  
  - \*[Full-Attuning]({% link obsidian/Attuning.md %}).  
  - [Assisting] on an action without casting a spell.   
  - \**Gathering Information:* [Specifying] a presented Threat. You may also use this action for any nontrivial information gathering. You may have to make an Action Roll to gain this information. 
  - *Exerting Yourself:* Completing a nontrivial physical task, such as feats of athletics or dexterity, provided that those tasks are plausible to complete without magical assistance. 
  - Using a \[Prepared\] action that did not have its condition met this round.   

Major Actions (2AP):  
  - Casting a Minor or Major Spell, for any reason.  
  - Making a nonmagical attack  
  - \*\[Preparing\] a 2AP action.   

Extreme Actions (4AP):  
  - Casting an Extreme Spell, for any reason.   
  - \*\[Preparing\] a 4AP action.
